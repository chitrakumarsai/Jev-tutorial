The agreement grants a 5% discount on all line-haul charges once cumulative annual Loads exceed 1,000. [C2] The discount begins with invoices issued for the calendar month after the threshold is first exceeded, rather than on the threshold-crossing month itself. [C3] Loads from January through May total 895; June adds 190, bringing the cumulative total to 1,085 and triggering the discount for July invoices. [C5] The same issue continues from August through December: the invoices show no reduction to line-haul charges. The resulting line-haul discount shortfall for July–December is $74,980.

The fuel surcharge also requires correction for the discounted months. Under the agreement it is calculated on the same invoice’s line-haul charges after any volume discount and cannot exceed 18%. In April, before the volume discount became effective, the carrier billed 22% of the $261,500 line-haul subtotal, or $57,530. [C6] The agreement caps the fuel surcharge at 25% of line-haul charges, so the April surcharge is within the cap. [C7] For July–December, applying each invoice’s stated percentage to the discounted line-haul base would reduce fuel surcharges by approximately $12,561. Thus, the estimated combined exposure for the volume discount and related fuel calculation is approximately $87,541, before considering detention. These are calculated amounts and should be confirmed against supporting records and any applicable index publication.

Detention must be pre-approved in writing by the Customer, and the carrier has not evidenced such approval. [C8] December bills $170 for a stated two-hour detention, although no time exceeds the two free hours. [C9] The February four-hour charge and May three-hour charge also appear to include the free hours; assuming the stated durations include all waiting time, those three invoices together appear to overstate detention by $510. The carrier should provide occurrence-level time records and explain its billing convention before any final adjustment is recorded.

The May invoice’s $4,075.08 late fee on the March invoice appears arithmetically consistent with 1.5% of $271,672. The stated payment date, 11 May, is more than 30 days after the 31 March invoice date, and the agreement permits such a fee to be billed later. [C10] I saw no repeat onboarding charge in the invoices reviewed. The monthly invoice dates and stated net-30 due dates are also generally consistent with the agreement; the supplied invoices alone do not establish when payments were made, other than the payment date stated for March.

I recommend that management request corrected invoices or credits for the July–December discount and surcharge amounts, and obtain supporting detention records. The agreement allows an annual audit with 15 business days’ notice and requires overbilled amounts identified in an audit to be credited within 30 days. Management should retain its recalculation and confirm the resulting credits against the carrier’s records.
